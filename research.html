Interests

I am broadly interested in water governance: how the way a water system is organized, financed, and managed shows up in its physical infrastructure and in the service that households actually receive at the tap. I care about the gap between how a system performs on paper and how it is experienced by the people who depend on it, and about the places, such as small and rural systems and fragmented sectors, where that gap tends to be widest.

I am currently exploring research directions in the Philippines, where water supply is delivered by a patchwork of water districts, local governments, private concessionaires, and community associations. I keep a comparative eye on small and rural systems in the United States, where I spent a year studying technology adoption and regionalization. My training is in engineering, and I want the work to stay anchored there: in measurable performance, in design decisions, and in tools people can use.

  • Governance and performanceHow institutional arrangements translate into pressure, quality, continuity, and cost at the household
  • Small and rural systemsTechnology adoption, technical assistance, consolidation and regionalization
  • Community-based methodsFieldwork rooted in residents' experience, paired with utility and audit records
  • Open toolsDashboards and web tools that make water and flood information plain and public

Past research

  1. Barriers to technology adoption in small and rural water systems

    Water Finance Exchange (WFX)Research fellow · WFX profile (opens in a new tab)

    A multi-stakeholder interview study across utility operators, technology vendors, consulting engineers, technical assistance providers, and policy experts, analyzed with matrix coding to surface why promising technologies stall before reaching small communities. Alongside it, a regionalization cost analysis for Tennessee's Duck River watershed, built from permit records and historical State Revolving Fund requests.

    2025 to 2026Washington, D.C. / remote

  2. Retrofit oxygenation technologies in wastewater treatment

    Boise State UniversityM.S. thesis, with Dr. Sondra Miller

    Feasibility and performance testing of a retrofittable high-concentration dissolved oxygen delivery system at a fully operational treatment plant: comprehensive sampling (BOD, COD, nitrogen, phosphorus, TSS, ATP), statistical and machine-learning analysis of the links among dissolved oxygen, biomass activity, and sludge settleability, and a look at the barriers between innovation and adoption.

    2023 to 2025Boise, Idaho

  3. Emerging contaminants across wastewater unit processes

    Boise State Universitywith Dr. Sondra Miller

    How Idaho's wastewater systems handle pharmaceuticals, PFAS, microplastics, and other unregulated pollutants: where policy lags, what utilities carry, and how ready the infrastructure is. The work proposes a framework to guide future policy and investment.

    2024 to 2026Boise, Idaho

Virtual fluids laboratories for CE 323

Four browser-based simulations of the Boise State CE 323 fluids experiments, built while I taught the lab. Each one walks through setup and calibration, lets you run the experiment and record readings, and then checks your own calculations against the simulated apparatus. They run entirely in the browser with no accounts or installs.
